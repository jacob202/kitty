### The eight-fact evidence contract

All eight render in `initiative status --json`: `blocked`,
`infrastructure_failures`, `worker_failed`, `operator_completed`,
`review_approved`, `pr_opened`, `done`, `exhausted`.

Two contract details are weaker than the packet's wording, both minor:

- **`blocked` carries no durable reason.** The contract asks for "the durable
  reason and next operator action." `next_action` exists but is derived from
  task state alone (`operator_review` / `complete` / `run`), not from why the
  packet actually blocked. The reason is recoverable from the event trail, so
  nothing is lost — it is just not in the rollup.
- **`counts_toward_budget` is derived, not persisted.** Budget neutrality is
  correct and proven, but it is computed from the attempt outcome rather than
  stored as a field. A future outcome added without updating
  `_BUDGET_CONSUMING_OUTCOMES` would silently start consuming budget.

## The gap that was here (closed by #350)

Kept as the record of what was wrong, because the shape of the fix only makes
sense against it.

Packet 026 requires that a worker failure followed by an operator completion
and an independent review render as three separate facts. The workflow itself
always worked. Reaching it did not: it required driving attempts by hand, while
the packet's own "visible demo" told operators to use `run-packet`.

Three behaviours closed the door:

1. **`run-packet` consumed the entire budget on failure.** Its repair loop
   retried until the budget was gone. `max_attempts: 3` plus a failing worker
   ended at 3/3 in one invocation, so raising the budget only fed the worker.
2. **A review cannot attach to a closed attempt.** After a worker fails its
   attempt closes `failed`, and `record-review` returns
   `error: attempt 6 is closed (failed)`. The operator's work needs an attempt
   of its own — the right model, not a bug.
3. **No verb performed the intervention.** `start-attempt` answered
   `has used N/N attempts; operator intervention required`, and `queue edit`
   offered `--title`, `--description`, `--priority`, `--acceptance`,
   `--allowed-paths` — no `--max-attempts`. The error named an action the tool
   did not have.

Reproduction, before the fix:

```bash
./kitty builder initiative run-packet <init> <packet> --worker-command '["false"]' --no-governor
./kitty builder initiative start-attempt <init> <packet>
# error: <init>/<packet> has used 1/1 attempts; operator intervention required
```

**Fix as landed (#350):** `./kitty builder initiative grant-attempt <init>
<packet> --reason "<why>"` raises `max_attempts` by exactly one and records a
durable `attempt_granted` event carrying the operator reason and the previous
and new limits. Prior attempts are never touched, so "never rewrite worker
history" holds and the error message is now true.

RP-07 drives the whole public path: worker failure → exhaustion →
`start-attempt` refuses → grant → new attempt → operator completion →
independent review approval, with the worker failure still reported at the end.
